# Interview guide: screening and referral workflow

**For:** E8-01. About 30 minutes. Semi-structured: follow the person, and use the questions as prompts, not a script.
**Before starting:** IRB answer recorded ([ethics-pathway.md](../../ethics/ethics-pathway.md)); written consent given; recording only if the person ticked the opt-in.

**Never** ask for, look at or accept patient images, records or names. If someone starts to share them, stop them kindly (Team Rules 8.1, 8.3).

## Opening (2 minutes)

"Thank you for your time. We are students building a phone app that could help health workers decide who to refer after a chest X-ray or an eye photo. We want to learn how your work happens today. There are no right or wrong answers. You can skip any question or stop at any time."

## 1. Role and setting (5 minutes)

1. What is your role, and what kind of facility do you work in? (Note the facility type only, not its name.)
2. On a typical day, how many people do you see who might need a chest X-ray or an eye check?
3. Who takes the X-rays or eye photos? Who reads them? How long does that take?

## 2. Today's workflow (8 minutes)

1. Walk me through the last time a patient needed a chest X-ray read. What happened, step by step? (No patient details, please.)
2. Where do X-rays end up: film, screen, printout, phone photo, WhatsApp? Where does the light come from when you look at film?
3. How do you decide who to refer, and where to? What slows that down?
4. For people with diabetes, how are eye checks done today? Is there a retinal camera?
5. What do you write down or record for each person? On paper, a register or a phone?

## 3. Devices and connectivity (5 minutes)

1. What phone do you use at work? Is it yours or the facility's? (Make and rough age only.)
2. How often is there mobile data or Wi-Fi at work? What happens when there is none?
3. Do you install apps for work? What makes you trust or stop using one?
4. Power: how often is there no electricity? How do you charge your phone?

## 4. Reading results (7 minutes)

Show the three result cards on paper (draw them from [low-fi-flows.md](low-fi-flows.md), screen 6). Read each one aloud; do not explain them. The icons are a proposal; the TRD only requires colour plus icon plus text (NFR-USAB-03).

> **Refer now** (red, arrow-to-clinic icon): "High risk on this scan. Refer for a TB confirmation test."
> **Not sure** (amber, question-mark icon): "Not sure. Retake the image, or refer."
> **Low risk** (green, down-arrow icon): "Low risk on this scan. This does not rule out disease. If symptoms continue, test or refer."
> Under each: "Screening support only. A clinician decides."

1. In your own words, what does each card tell you to do?
2. What would you do next for a person who got the amber card? And the green one?
3. Is anything confusing, or likely to be misread by a busy colleague?
4. If the app said the photo was not clear enough and asked for a retake, how would that fit into your day?

5. Which icon would you expect for each card? (A check mark on green may read as "all clear"; ask, do not suggest.)

Listen for anyone reading green as "the person is fine". That is the false-reassurance hazard (H1, H2): note their exact words.

## 5. Wrap-up (3 minutes)

1. If this app could do one thing really well for you, what should it be?
2. What would stop you from using it?
3. Would you be willing to try a prototype in late November or December? (Only note yes or no. Contact details go to private storage.)

"Thank you. We will not use your name anywhere."
